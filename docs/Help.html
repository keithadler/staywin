Where you stand

Windows 10 stopped being supported on 14 October 2025. Extended Security Updates carry on to 12 October 2027, and you can still join at any time until then. There are three ways in and one of them costs nothing: sync your PC settings to a Microsoft account, spend 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points, or pay $30 once for up to ten PCs on the same account. Stay takes you to the page; it cannot enrol you, because that is a Microsoft account flow inside Settings.

ESU is critical and important security updates for Windows itself. Not bug fixes, not features, not support, and nothing for any other program.

Entitled is not the same as receiving. Stay reads the day an update really installed and compares it to what your PC is entitled to. An enrolment that stopped delivering is worse than none, because you believe you are covered. Anything wrong is listed with why it matters and what to do.

Whether you are enrolled is worked out from two independent things: the licences Windows holds, and whether anything has installed since support ended — which on an unenrolled PC nothing can. When they agree, Stay says so and says why. When they disagree it tells you they disagree and shows you both, rather than guessing. Telling somebody who is enrolled that they are not is the worst mistake it could make.

Your browser is on this page too. Almost everything that gets onto a PC arrives through the browser, so on a Windows nobody is patching, the browser matters more than every switch in this app put together — and it is still being updated. Microsoft has committed to Edge until October 2028. Google has published no end date for Chrome on Windows 10. Mozilla has said there is no end date for Firefox. Where nobody has promised anything, Stay says that rather than inventing a date.

Windows 11

Stay checks the same things Microsoft's own tool does and tells you which check fails, because the answers are not alike. A TPM switched off in firmware is ten minutes in the BIOS. A processor that is not on the list is a new PC. Stay will not get around the checks and will not tell you how.

What to shut

Fifteen doors worth closing on a PC nobody is patching. Each says what it is, what shutting it does, and what it costs you. Two that are most likely to get in the way — Remote Desktop and the print spooler — are not ticked for you.

It cannot make an unpatched Windows safe. Nothing can except Microsoft. What this reduces is how much of the PC is reachable.

Make it faster

Windows times its own start, and times the programs holding it up, in a log almost nobody knows exists. Stay reads it, so instead of "High" or "Medium" you get OneDrive, 4.2 seconds at sign-in. Stopping one does not remove the program; it opens normally when you open it. Startup programs are never ticked for you.

Then five switches that genuinely help: animations and shadows, transparency, the search index on a spinning disk, SysMain on a solid one, and Storage Sense so the disk stops filling. It is a short list on purpose — most of what circulates as Windows tuning is folklore.

It does not make Windows 10 fast. If the PC has a spinning hard disk, an SSD would do more for it than everything in this app put together, and Stay says so before offering you a single switch. Space that could come back is shown but never deleted: deleting is the one thing a receipt cannot undo, so Stay opens Windows' own cleanup instead.

Turn off the junk

The advertising, the AI hooks, the telemetry and the noise, and the bundled apps nobody asked for. The switch list comes from Quiet for Windows, which is written for Windows 11 — so eight of its switches are for features Windows 10 never had. Those are left out, and Stay says how many and why: writing a value that does nothing would leave you believing you had turned something off.

Removing an app is the only thing Stay does that a receipt cannot undo. It says exactly that before the button, not after, and the receipt keeps a Store link for each one.

What turned itself back on

Windows puts its own defaults back when it updates itself and does not mention it. Nobody remembers what they switched off six months ago — but the receipts do, so Stay checks every value it ever set and tells you what has come back, when you turned it off, and offers to do it again.

Receipts

Every change Stay has made on this PC, with the value each thing had before, and one button that puts it back. Receipts live in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Stay as plain JSON you can read. Stay can also ask Windows for a restore point first, which is a tick box on the bar.

The console twin

stay.exe is the same program without a window, with --json on everything.

stay status                  where this PC stands
stay patched                 what is still getting updates, and until when
stay eleven                  whether this PC can take Windows 11
stay guards                  what is open that this app can shut
stay harden --all            shut everything it suggests, with a receipt
stay harden --all --dry-run  show the exact changes and make none
stay speed                   what is making this PC slow
stay speed --all             do everything it suggests for speed
stay speed --stop OneDrive   stop one program starting with the PC
stay junk                    the advertising, AI hooks and telemetry still on
stay junk --all              turn all of it off
stay junk --apps             remove the bundled apps the list calls junk
stay drift                   what you turned off that has turned itself back on
stay drift --fix             turn all of it off again
stay receipts                every change ever made
stay undo <id>               put one receipt's changes back
stay report                  everything, as one document
stay selftest                the checks, from inside the shipped exe

Exit codes: 0 fine, 1 something to look at, 2 a problem, 64 a mistake in the command.

Privacy

No account, no cloud, no telemetry, nothing about you or this PC leaves it. The one network request is a daily question to GitHub about whether there is a newer version, which sends no identifier and which you can turn off.
